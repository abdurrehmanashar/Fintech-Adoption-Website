How the study was built

A transparent path from survey data to segmentation, predictive modelling, validation, and interpretation.

Global Findex

Pakistan sample with measures of digital access, financial behaviour, demographics and inclusion.

02 - DISCOVERY

Segmentation

k-means clustering identified population profiles; Ward hierarchical clustering provided a cross-check.

03 - PREDICTION

ML models

Model performance

How predictive are the models?

Metrics from the tuned, cross-validated models reported in the paper. Macro F1 is used throughout since the adoption tiers are substantially imbalanced (68.2% Excluded vs. 2.1% Basic) - accuracy alone can look strong while badly underperforming on smaller classes.

ModelMacro F1
Random Forest (tuned)0.494
XGBoost0.481

Repeated cross-validation (50 folds) confirms the Random Forest estimate is stable (mean 0.480, 95% CI 0.447-0.521). XGBoost separately achieved 80.0% raw accuracy - a number that looks strong but is inflated by the imbalanced target, which is exactly why macro F1 is the metric prioritized here.

A finding worth reading carefully

Why "the model is fairer to women" would be the wrong conclusion

A direct check of the model's own behaviour turned up something that matters more than it first looks like.

A

The raw numbers

  • 85.2% accuracy for women
  • 68.9% accuracy for men
Read on their own, this looks like the model works better for women.
B

Why that's misleading

  • Gender and outcome are strongly entangled (Cramer's V = 0.807)
  • Most women fall into the largest, easiest-to-predict class
Higher accuracy here reflects class imbalance, not fairer treatment.
C

What we recommend instead

  • Report subgroup-level macro F1, not aggregate accuracy
  • Applies to similar financial-inclusion models generally
Not a Pakistan-specific issue - a general risk wherever a demographic variable is entangled with outcome.

A second, related check: the model's predicted probabilities are poorly calibrated for the Excluded class - when it assigns roughly 40% probability to someone being excluded, the true observed rate is closer to 65-70%. In practice, this means the model systematically underestimates how many people are excluded when its raw probabilities are used directly, rather than its hard predictions. Anyone using a model like this for resource targeting would need to correct for that before trusting the numbers.

Research integrity

What this study cannot tell us

The strongest claims are often the ones that clearly define their boundaries.

Association is not causation

The analysis identifies relationships but does not prove intervention effects.

Cross-sectional data

The study captures respondents at one point in time and cannot establish trajectories.

Self-reported information

Several measures rely on reported behaviour and inherit survey-based limits.

Context matters

Results describe this Pakistan sample and should not be generalized automatically.

What comes next

Questions the next study should answer

  • Longitudinal data: how do people move between segments?
  • Qualitative research: why are connected people not engaging?
  • Causal methods: which interventions actually change behaviour?
  • Cross-country replication: is the access-usage gap universal?
  • Intervention testing: what closes the gap in practice?
From finding to action

What could these findings lead to?

These are hypotheses for further research, not proven policy outcomes.

66.8% remain excluded despite connectivity

Possible explanation

Trust, onboarding friction, costs, or behavioural barriers may matter alongside access.

Possible intervention

Test targeted digital-finance education and simpler onboarding.

96.3% of the excluded cluster are women

Possible explanation

Participation may be shaped by barriers not captured by connectivity alone.

Possible intervention

Design and evaluate women-focused access and literacy programmes.

Access is weaker than behaviour in explaining engagement

Research question

Can interventions that change saving or participation behaviour improve inclusion?

What to test

Move toward longitudinal and intervention-based research.
