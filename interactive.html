Gender and urban/rural

How adoption splits by group

Both associations are statistically significant - but at very different effect sizes.

Gender

The strongest structural predictor

Cluster membership: chi-square = 650.549, p < 0.0001, Cramer's V = 0.807. The most excluded cluster is 96.3% female; the most advanced cluster is 10.3% female.

Urban / Rural

A real but smaller effect

Adoption Tier: chi-square = 21.742, p = 0.0001, Cramer's V = 0.147. Rural share declines from 51.0% in the least-adopted cluster to 28.6% in the most-adopted.

Gini and SHAP largely agree; permutation importance — which measures actual performance degradation — agrees only weakly, suggesting some features' importance comes from correlation with other predictors rather than standalone necessity.

Spearman rank correlations: Gini vs SHAP ρ = 0.914 (p < 0.0001); Permutation vs SHAP ρ = 0.504 (p = 0.0557, not significant at α=0.05). Permutation importance is negative for several features (con1, female, internet_use, fin24a, educ, is_rural), meaning shuffling those features didn't reliably hurt model performance.

How predictive are the models?

Metrics from the tuned, cross-validated models reported in the paper.

ModelMacro F1
Random Forest (tuned)0.494
XGBoost0.481

Macro F1 is prioritized over accuracy given class imbalance (68.2% Excluded vs. 2.1% Basic). Repeated cross-validation (50 folds) confirms the Random Forest estimate is stable (mean 0.480, 95% CI 0.447-0.521). XGBoost separately achieved 80.0% raw accuracy - a number that looks strong but is inflated by the imbalanced target, which is exactly why macro F1 is the metric prioritized here.
